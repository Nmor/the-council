# Economics calculations

State currency, period, cohort and data source beside every input. Label projections.
Preserve provenance: observed spend, a quote and a projection are different evidence.
An experiment must observe its metric within its time cap; if renewals fall later,
schedule a later retention checkpoint instead of claiming proof within a shorter pilot.

- Unit contribution = net unit receipts less variable delivery, payment and support costs.
  State whether acquisition cost is included; do not count it twice.
- Break-even units = fixed costs / positive unit contribution for the same period.
  Non-positive contribution has no finite volume-based break-even under those assumptions.
- Required unit contribution at a target volume = fixed costs / target units for the same
  period. Recalculate contribution and break-even for every proposed price/cost threshold;
  a cost reduction can improve economics without reaching the claimed viability target.
- Net monthly cash burn = monthly cash outflows less cash inflows. Simple runway =
  available unrestricted cash / positive net burn. Include committed one-off spending
  and changing burn in a cash forecast. Zero/negative burn is not a finite runway estimate.
- CAC = attributable acquisition spend / newly acquired paying customers in the same
  acquisition population. Explain excluded spend, delayed conversions and channel mix.
- Payback = CAC / monthly contribution per retained customer only under stated stable
  assumptions. Model churn and collection timing when material. Zero/negative contribution
  cannot yield meaningful payback through this formula.
  Payback recovery and contribution toward fixed costs are separate calculations.
- Contribution LTV = expected retained-period contributions less applicable lifecycle costs.
  Revenue/churn shortcuts require stable cohorts and periods; report immature cohorts and
  avoid a precise long-term LTV from a few observations. Keep CAC separate if comparing LTV/CAC.

Do not infer retention from sign-ups, revenue from a pipeline, or margin from list price.
For a prepayment business, model fulfillment liabilities and cash timing explicitly.
