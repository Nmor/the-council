# Sampling and findings

Define the unit, full population, period and control frequency before choosing samples.
Verify population completeness using an independent source where available. Record
sampling method, rationale, sample size, exclusions and reproducible selection details.

Risk-based/judgmental samples can find exceptions but do not support statistical rates
for the population. Statistical inference requires a suitable sampling design, confidence
and tolerable error assumptions. Do not invent a universal sample count or claim that
zero exceptions in a small sample proves universal compliance.

Test each selected item against the control criteria and retain a redacted evidence key.
Differentiate control failed, evidence missing, item outside scope and test not executed.
For an automated control, examine change control and configuration for the period as well
as representative execution. A newly enabled check cannot prove prior-period operation.

Describe conclusions at their actual boundary: design adequate/inadequate, implemented/
not implemented, operating effective/ineffective for the tested scope, or insufficient
evidence. A failed instance does not prove inadequate control design; assess the design
separately before assigning that conclusion. State scope/sample limitations next to the
conclusion. A management readiness assessment is not an independent assurance opinion.

Use a findings table when useful: risk/control, criteria, condition, evidence key, effect,
cause confidence, priority, owner, due date and retest evidence. Do not fill unknown causes
with speculation or assign a deadline/owner as if agreed when they are only proposed.
